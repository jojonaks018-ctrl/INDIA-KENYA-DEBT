01 THE FINDING

Absolute debt tells one story. Debt relative to economic size tells another.

India and Kenya have very different-sized economies. That matters when comparing their external debt.

In absolute dollars, India's external debt was 16.7 times Kenya's in 2024. But relative to GDP, Kenya had the higher ratio: 35.6% compared with 19.1% for India.

WHY THE MEASURE MATTERS

A large economy can carry a much larger debt stock in dollar terms without that debt representing the same share of economic output. Scaling debt to GDP makes the size of the two economies part of the comparison.

THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A country can rank very high in total external debt because its economy is large. Absolute debt therefore tells only part of the story.

08 WHAT THE RATIO DOES NOT TELL US

A higher debt-to-GDP ratio does not by itself determine debt sustainability.

The ratio answers one specific question: how large the external debt stock is compared with the size of the economy.

KENYA 35.6%
INDIA 19.1%

The wider debt picture requires more data.

Interest costs, maturities, currencies, foreign-exchange earnings, reserves, government revenue and loan terms can all affect how manageable a debt stock is.

THE NEXT QUESTIONS

Who owes the debt? In which currencies? At what cost? When is it due? And what generates the foreign currency needed to service it?

09 METHODOLOGY & DATA

The same calculation is applied to both countries.

Source

External debt figures come from the World Bank International Debt Statistics, indicator DT.DOD.DECT.CD — External debt stocks, total (current US$) .

GDP is nominal GDP in current US dollars. The same approach is used for both countries.

Calculation

External debt ÷ GDP × 100

India: $716.5B ÷ $3.76T × 100 = 19.1%

Kenya: $42.9B ÷ $120.4B × 100 = 35.6%

WHAT THIS MEASURE MEANS

The external-debt stock is a broad measure of debt owed to non-residents. It should not be interpreted as government debt alone. A higher debt-to-GDP ratio also does not, by itself, establish that a country faces a debt crisis.
