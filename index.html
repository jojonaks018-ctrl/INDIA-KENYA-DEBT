Kenya’s Smaller External Debt Carries a Heavier Burden than India’s

Kenya owes far less than India in absolute terms, but its external debt represents a much larger share of national income.

Even though Kenya has a significantly lower total amount of external debt than India, its external debt represents a significantly larger share of national income. Over the past ten years, the difference between the two countries' debt burdens has widened.

According to data from the World Bank, the ratio of external debt to Gross National Income (GNI) increased for Kenya from 27.9% in 2014 to 35% in 2024, while India's ratio decreased from 22.7% to 18.6%. By 2024, Kenya's external debt was nearly twice as large relative to national income as India's.

Throughout most of the ten-year period, Kenya experienced an increase in its debt burden. At its highest point in 2023, Kenya's external debt reached 40.4% of GNI. In 2024, the ratio fell to 35% as Kenya's national income increased while its external debt remained relatively stable.

Although Kenya's external debt has grown significantly since 2014, the growth of national income has not been sufficient to prevent external debt from representing a larger share of GNI. India, by contrast, saw its external debt-to-GNI ratio decline over the same period.

The size of Kenya's external debt is also reflected in the cost of servicing it. In 2024, external debt service amounted to 27.2% of Kenya's export earnings. Kenya therefore needs a steady flow of foreign-exchange earnings to meet its external debt obligations.

The creditors Kenya owes money to have also changed. An increasing percentage of Kenya's external debt is now owed to multilateral organizations, while the share owed to private creditors has declined. Multilateral creditors accounted for 46.6% of Kenya's external debt in 2024, compared with 36.3% in 2019.

India represents a clear contrast with Kenya regarding debt burdens. India has a much higher total amount of external debt, but its external debt has become a smaller proportion of national income over the same period that Kenya's has become larger.

The two countries began the ten-year period with relatively close debt-to-GNI ratios. Kenya's ratio was only about five percentage points higher than India's in 2014. By 2024, the difference had grown to more than 16 percentage points.

Although Kenya's debt burden improved slightly in 2024, it did not alter the longer-term trend identified in the data. Kenya moved down from its recent peak without significantly reducing the amount of external debt it owed.

For Kenya, the longer-term question is whether economic growth can continue to outpace the growth of external debt, allowing the burden to become a smaller share of national income.

Sources & methodology

Data: World Bank International Debt Statistics. External debt burden is measured using external debt stock as a percentage of Gross National Income (GNI). Debt service is measured separately as a share of exports of goods, services and primary income.
